01 — Territory
Nature & the artificial

Don't invent another world.
Work with this one.

Herbert A. Simon asked us to study not only how things are, but how things ought to be to attain a goal. A bridge, an instrument, a computer program: each is artificial, yet each must obey the world it enters.

One string can make sound. Frets and six strings arrange what sound already permits so that the human hand can play. That arrangement is neither the physics nor the music. It is the instrument.

Understand the nature of the problem. Work around it.

A design earns its existence only where it changes the outcome. Explanation can tell you why a spinning gyroscope resists your hand; it cannot replace holding the wheel.

Six encounters. Not six principles. Each records the moment a system answered a question differently than expected.

FIELD NOTE 001

The correction was the knowledge.

“Write hello world.” A model produces a command line. The developer wanted a playable page, but discovers that only after seeing the wrong shape. He corrects it; the model builds the page. Reset the machine and the same first ask makes the same command line. The correction never crossed the reset.
