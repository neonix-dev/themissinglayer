01 The Crossing

Two sides. One crossing. Only meaning travels.

Wherever two sides meet — a person and a system, a maker and a material, one mind and another — each side knows only its own interior. Everything else is a model, a crossing, and a cost. This is the shape of that meeting.

  • 1·5The two sides. Each knows only its own interior — nothing else on either side is given.
  • 2·4Each side’s model of the other. What A believes B can do; what B believes A can do. A model is not the side.
  • 3Knows what each side can do. Capability, held above the crossing — before anything happens.
  • 6The place that knows what each side does. The record of the act itself, where the apexes meet.
  • UThe interface. The curves underneath: the cost of carrying something across, paid on every crossing.
  • —First principles. The ground line both sides stand on, whether they know it or not.

02 First Principles

The ground line, named.

1

Environment — what does not move.

The unmoving frame. It has no oughts; it just is. You do not negotiate with it, model it away, or wait for it to change its mind. You build on it precisely because it will still be there.

“I don’t have to worry if the landing zone will be there after I jump and before I land.”
2

Fields of Interaction — what holds across a gap without touching it.

Interaction across separation, never by contact or merging. Two sides can act on each other across a distance — through a field, through an interface — while remaining exactly what they are. The gap is not a defect. The gap is where the field lives.

03 Three Definitions

Words, held to their bounds.

1

Artificial — produced by art rather than by nature.

The decided. Shared meaning is sometimes found, sometimes made — “A” is A, but “g” is whatever value we decide. An artificial system is one held to its oughts and bounds; testing is exactly that holding.

2

Artifact as Interface — after Herbert Simon.

An artifact is the meeting point between an inner — the substance and organization of the artifact itself — and an outer — the environment it operates in. The interface is the cost of carrying something across, and the component that transcribes meaning across. Only the meaning travels.

3

Practice — the generator itself, running.

Solving once and again; each “again” deposits confidence. Practice is not preparation for the real thing — run in the present tense, it is the real thing. Mastery is what alignment looks like when the crossing is no longer needed.

04 The Instrument

Triangulation: do we know what each other knows?

The matrix exists for one purpose: to find out if you know what I know and I know what you know. It is only for the uncertain — things both sides are sure of never enter it. They are the checklist; they just run. Bring one uncertain question. Let each side answer, honestly, with its confidence. Watch the waves.

Rules of the matrix

  1. Only the uncertain enters. What both sides are sure of is the checklist — it just runs, and never takes up the matrix.
  2. Every question needs a floor and an exit. A floor: it must bottom out in what is. An exit: it resolves, or it graduates to protocol.
  3. Canon forms past a set threshold. The threshold is decided, not found — held like any other bound of an artificial system.
  4. At +1 the crossing is eliminated. Fully shared, the question dissolves — or becomes protocol, and runs without being asked.
  5. Canon has a shelf life. Even +1 expires. A parachute reserve packing is valid six months; then the checklist runs again.
